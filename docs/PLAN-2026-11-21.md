## Part 1. The research, explained

### 1.1 What goes wrong in roadside and home-service apps

Sources: complaint sites, review pages, news reports and tribunal decisions. Each one is a
complaint or an anecdote, so this is a list of things to design against, not measured rates.

| # | Problem | What actually happens | Sahayak now |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | The bill grows after the work | The mechanic names a price once the driver is stuck. A 5 km tow quoted at ₹3,000-8,000 | Price shown before sending. Above estimate +10% the partner must ask in the app and the owner approves **before** completion (ADR-039) |
| F2 | The start code is misused | Technicians start without the OTP, or ask for it before finishing; OTPs read out over the phone confirm a handover that never happened | Code works only within 1 km of the pickup (ADR-040). There is no code at completion, on purpose |
| F3 | Nobody comes, or the ETA keeps moving | Waits of 2-5 hours after prepaying | The search widens for 10 minutes. Once a partner is well past the ETA the owner gets "Find another partner", and the late partner is released (ADR-041) |
| F4 | "The tow damaged my car" | Tow operator says the damage was already there; photos decide these cases | Pickup and drop photos are required on tows (ADR-042) |
| F5 | "Cancel in the app and pay me cash" | Drivers skip the platform to avoid commission | Report reason "asked to pay outside the app", allowed on cancelled jobs, plus staff alerts on risky partners (ADR-043) |
| F6 | Fake helpers | Unsolicited tow trucks and callers posing as the company | App copy: only the partner shown in the app is from Sahayak; share the code only face to face |
| F7 | Wrong pin | GPS slightly off | Owner drags the pin and types a landmark; the partner calls |
| F8 | Partners blocked without a reason | Urban Company partner protests | Every rejection and suspension carries a reason in the audit log |
| F9 | Fake helpline numbers in search results | Scammers buy ads | Needs the real number published (R1) |
| F10 | No signal on the highway | Request lost | Resent for three minutes without creating duplicates (ADR-044) |

**Where each step goes.** Phone OTP when sending (guests can browse prices first) → pin → price
→ start code at the vehicle → extra charges approved during the job → tow photos at pickup and
drop → completion at the agreed price → payment confirmed by whoever received it → ratings both
ways.

### 1.2 Who else does this

**Running**
* **ReadyAssist** (Bengaluru): the closest match. About $6-7M raised, FY24 revenue about ₹12 Cr
  against ₹23 Cr costs, so still losing money after about ten years. Mostly sold through
  subscriptions, carmakers, insurers and fleets.
* **GoMechanic**: car servicing, now also roadside repair. Survived a collapse (below).
* **Urban Company**: the home-services model that worked: vetted, trained partners and fixed
  prices. Listed in 2025. The FY25 "profit" was mostly a one-time tax credit; the operating
  profit was thin.
* **HONK** (US): began as a consumer app; now mostly runs roadside help behind insurers' brands.
* **Wrench / YourMechanic** (US): mobile mechanics with upfront prices, and a steady stream of
  refund and quality complaints.

**In trouble or shut down, and why**
* **GoMechanic, 2023**: inflated revenue and garages that did not exist. It laid off 70% of
  staff, its valuation fell from $285M to about $30M, it was sold off cheaply, and police filed
  a case against the founders.
* **Urgently** (US, listed): falling revenue as big customers left, listing warnings, debt.
* **Autto.in** (doorstep car service): customers came back only every 10-12 months, so it kept
  paying to win them again. It expanded too fast and mechanics quit.
* **Taskbob, Doormint, Tooler** (home services): could not raise more money; refunds and rework
  ate the margin; too many identical rivals.
* **Homejoy** (US): customers bought with discounts did not return; legal fights over how its
  workers were classified.

**The five lessons**
1. A breakdown is rare for any one driver, so a consumer-only app keeps paying to acquire
   customers. The survivors sell through insurers, fleets, carmakers and subscriptions.
2. Partner supply decides everything. No partners nearby means "no partner found", and that
   owner does not come back.
3. Never fake supply or numbers. That is what broke GoMechanic.
4. Disputes and refunds are everyday work, not an edge case.
5. Grow one area at a time, at the speed the checks allow.

### 1.3 Laws and store rules that apply

| Rule | What it says | What it means for Sahayak | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Play user-data policy | Any app that lets people create an account must let them delete it in the app, and give a web link that explains how | Both roles must be able to delete | **Done** (owners ADR-025, partners ADR-045, web page `/delete-account`) |
| Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 | Show a grievance officer's name, designation and contact; acknowledge complaints in 48 hours and resolve them within a month | A real, named person is needed | Section built; **the name is a placeholder** |
| DPDP Act 2023 + Rules notified 13 Nov 2025 | Notice and consent, security, breach reporting, deletion, retention. The main duties apply from about 13 May 2027 | Time to prepare, but design for it now | Export and deletion done; consent notice, breach runbook and retention schedule to do |
| Code on Social Security (in force 21 Nov 2025) | The government may charge platforms 1-2% of turnover (capped at 5% of payouts to gig workers) for a welfare fund | A cost to build into prices | **Not handled**: needs a CA |
| Karnataka Platform Based Gig Workers Act, 2025 | Platforms register and pay a welfare fee of 1-5% of each payout | Applies in Bengaluru, where the map opens by default | **Not handled**: needs a CA |

5. **Growth:** fleet and insurer accounts, scheduled jobs, subscriptions.
